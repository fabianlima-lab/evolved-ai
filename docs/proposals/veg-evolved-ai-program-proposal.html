02 · Why

Why this matters

Help people stay in the profession longer.

Emergency medicine asks more of our people than almost any corner of vet med: overnights, compassion fatigue, decision load, and the administrative weight that follows everyone home.

  • Burnout and attrition are the profession's defining retention problem — and ER shifts concentrate both.
  • Mental-health risk in veterinary medicine is well documented; support built by someone who understands the work gets used — generic corporate wellness programs don’t.
  • The cost of losing one trained ER doctor or nurse — recruiting, credentialing, ramp-up — dwarfs the cost of a wellness program for an entire hospital.

The goal is simple: reduce the daily load that drives burnout, so VEGgies stay healthier, happier, and at VEG longer. Even if it only helps one, it's worth doing — but it's built to help many.

05 · The Model

The model

Similar playbook as the large-animal training partnership.

Similar to the farm-training program: an initiative, run independently, that gives VEGgies extra support at cost because of its impact.

  • Bethany owns and operates the program. It is an independent initiative, not a VEG product.
  • A 99% reduced cost, not the full price. VEG pays part of the expenses to develop & run the program for its people — no margin, no markup.
  • One membership. All VEGgies. Membership is open to every VEGgie — one flat sponsorship covers them all.
  • Outside sales stay separate. The public-facing product continues independently and will never present itself as VEG-sponsored or VEG-branded.
  • An ongoing partnership. Open-ended terms, reviewed together regularly — either side can propose adjustments as the program and enrollment grow.
